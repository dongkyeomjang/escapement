\section{What runtime policy cannot reach}
\label{sec:05}

\subsection{There is headroom, and utilization does not measure it}

An offline bound over return schedules finds real room: with a budget of 2 s under the measured tool-latency distribution, 5.0--6.9 \% of device time. The bound comes from local search and is therefore a \textbf{lower} bound.
% CLAIMS 2.3
% CLAIMS 2.2

Before using it we have to discard the metric this project had been judging by. Under a rescheduling that preserves total work, slot occupancy and device time move in \textbf{opposite} directions: the schedule that maximises utilization spends 11--37 \% more device time, and in measured arm comparisons the two disagree in direction in 5 of 11 cells. Utilization remains a valid observable; it is not a cost function.
% CLAIMS 2.1

\subsection{What a causal policy does with it}

The offline optimum does not hold sessions back evenly. It releases most of them immediately and detains a few --- and \emph{which} few depends on when the others will return. Every causal policy we evaluated, seeing only the present state, produced a negative mean saving; the best was $-$0.44 \%. On device this reproduced: the recovery fraction was $-$105 \% and $-$70 \% in the two confirmation cells --- the policy increased device time rather than reducing it. \textbf{The intuition "wait a moment and go together" is not merely unhelpful here; it costs about as much as the headroom is worth.}
% CLAIMS 2.4
% CLAIMS 2.4

\subsection{Most of the headroom is coordination, and knowledge cannot buy it}

We decompose the headroom by information rather than by policy. If every session is given \textbf{omniscient} knowledge of the future but each is required to decide \textbf{independently}, 27--51 \% of the bound is recovered, and the remaining \textbf{median 60 \% (range 49--73 \%) disappears}.
% CLAIMS 2.5

That share is not lost to ignorance. It is lost to the decision structure: when the gain comes from several sessions riding the same batch, \emph{which} group forms cannot be decided by any one session from its own information. \textbf{No per-session runtime policy can reach it, in principle.} The only mechanisms that can achieve coordination are a scheduler that sees every session, or a configuration that freezes the coordination at build time for everybody.
% CLAIMS 2.6

\subsection{And the reachable remainder is not reachable in practice}

Two independent reasons close it.

\textbf{The value of return-time information is workload-specific.} Under a synthetic gap law, knowing exactly when peers return recovers 86--88 \% of the bound and defines an accuracy threshold of the same order as the gap standard deviation. Under the \emph{measured} tool-latency distribution the same information is worth $-$1.20 \% to +0.99 \%, and no threshold is definable. The opportunity to co-schedule is similar in both (73.6 \% vs 81.9 \% at a 2 s budget) --- \textbf{it is not the opportunity that is missing but its value.}
% CLAIMS 2.7

\textbf{And the prediction is not available anyway.} Borrowing the tool-duration estimator of \cite{continuum2025} verbatim, the converged error exceeds the accuracy threshold by 5.6--9.7$\times$, and growing per-tool samples by four orders of magnitude moves the error standard deviation from 8.4 s to 10.5 s --- in the wrong direction. The bound and the point estimate hit the same wall (10.196 vs 10.185 s), which locates the problem: not a weaker estimator but weaker \textbf{conditioning}. Whether \texttt{Bash} takes 27 ms or 300 s is decided by the command, and the tool name does not carry the command.

\subsection{A methodological warning that applies to us too}

Two free parameters and a twenty-configuration search manufacture a +4.32 \% gain on exploration seeds that becomes $-$0.14 \% on held-out seeds; an earlier instance with a larger search showed up to 34 \% before the sign reversed. Everything in Section~\ref{sec:06} is therefore selected on one set of seeds, evaluated on another, and confirmed against predictions committed before measurement.
